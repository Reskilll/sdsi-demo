Nitro: AI agents that turn a prospect's website into a personalised pitch in minutes

The problem
Pre-sales teams struggle to reach new clients. Before we can approach a prospect, someone senior has to study their website, work out their technology stack, find which of our people are free, and write a proposal. That takes days, so we rarely reach out at all. The few proposals we do send are generic: they talk about our years of experience and our skills, but nothing in them shows that we looked at the client's own business. They seldom convert.
At the same time, skilled developers wait between projects. That time is a real cost to the organisation, and the client never sees what these people could do. In meetings with CTOs we can only describe our skills. We cannot show, right then, what we would fix on their site.

The idea
What if the prospect could see their own website, improved, before they signed anything? Nitro makes that possible. Paste a prospect's website address and a team of AI agents goes to work. In a few minutes it produces a report card on the site, a map of its technology, a redesigned homepage, a matched team of our available experts, and a personalised pitch deck and proposal. A human reviews everything before anything is sent.

How it works
Nitro has 11 agents, each with one job. The Coordinator plans the run and decides the next step. The Site Auditor opens the site in a real browser. The Speed Tester runs Lighthouse for page speed and Core Web Vitals. The Mobile Tester checks the page at phone size. The Accessibility Checker runs axe-core against WCAG rules. The UX Reviewer looks at a screenshot and scores it against a fixed rubric. The AI-Search Checker checks whether AI assistants can find and quote the site. The Tech Detector reads the page's network traffic, headers and cookies to find the front end, back end, CMS, commerce platform, analytics, CDN and cloud in use. The Designer takes the prospect's own colours, fonts and content and builds an improved homepage in HTML, then has it re-checked by the same tests. The Team Matcher finds our available experts with the right skills. The Deck Writer writes a PowerPoint pitch deck and a written proposal.
A run takes about 1.5 to 3 minutes in our tests, and the app can run up to three at the same time. Every step streams live to the screen, and every run is saved so it can be replayed for free.

Real results, not promises
On a real run against allbirds.com, Nitro scored the homepage 64 out of 100 (grade D), with speed, mobile, accessibility, UX and AI-search scores each backed by evidence. From 389 network requests it detected 9 technologies, found that 49% of the traffic was third-party across 25 domains, and flagged Shopify as a skill gap for us instead of hiding it. The redesigned homepage raised accessibility from 70 to 98 and mobile from 87 to 100, re-measured by the same tools. It has also run on stripe.com and Samsung India.

Insights across the whole stack
One address gives a full-stack view: the web front end (frameworks, layout, mobile, accessibility, UX), the back end (server languages, CMS and commerce platforms), DevOps and cloud (CDN, hosting, security and cache headers), performance (Lighthouse and Core Web Vitals) and AI readiness (structured data, llms.txt, AI-search visibility). Every insight is mapped to the people who can deliver it.

AI speed, combined with our developers
The agents do the heavy lifting: crawling, measuring, detecting the stack, scoring, redesigning and drafting. Our experts fine-tune the result: they check every finding, add real project know-how, adjust the plan, team and price, and own the conversation with the CTO. When a run finishes, it can be assigned to an available developer with the right skills to review and sign off, with their name on the pitch. Time between projects becomes useful, visible work, and developers learn new stacks from real client sites.

Reaching CTOs, even in a live meeting
Because a run takes minutes, we can paste a CTO's website address during a meeting and show the score, technology map and redesign on screen while we are still talking. The conversation moves from what we can do in general to what we would fix for them, with a named team. The next day they receive a deck reviewed by an expert.

An agent marketplace
Nitro includes an agent marketplace built into the app: one catalogue of every agent, what it takes in, what it produces and how to showcase it. Teams can find and reuse agents on their own accounts instead of rebuilding them, and sales can show clients the agents by category. It lists 23 agents today: 11 live, 1 in beta and 11 planned. Planned agents include a Journey Runner that uses the site like a real customer, a Test Writer that turns those journeys into automated Playwright tests, a Locale Tester for languages and right-to-left layouts, an HTML Fixer, a simple RAG chat builder made from the prospect's own pages, and Night Watch, which re-checks prospects 24x7 and alerts sales when a site slows down or relaunches.

Why it can be trusted
Tools measure, models judge, humans decide. Speed, accessibility, traffic and technologies are measured by real tools. The model (Claude) plans the work, judges design and clarity, and designs; code guardrails enforce the order of steps, retry limits and a step budget. If there is no model key or network, an offline mode still runs end to end. Nothing is sent to a client without human approval. Sample data, such as the staff list used for matching today, is clearly labelled until real HR data is connected.

Impact and what we will measure
Nitro lets us analyse many more prospects, reach CTOs with personalised proof, and put developers between projects to work. We will not claim numbers we have not measured. We will track proposals sent, replies from CTOs, conversions, revenue from new clients, talent utilisation and the time from first contact to project start.
